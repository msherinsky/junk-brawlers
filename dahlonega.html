Places People Buy to Come To, Not Live In.

Dahlonega has a kind of property most towns on our route do not: destination homes, used part of the year.

Montaluce, Achasta, and the Estate Communities

Montaluce is a Tuscan-styled community built around a working vineyard with its own restaurant, which is not a thing you find twice in North Georgia. Achasta covers 1,100 acres along the Chestatee with a Jack Nicklaus course through it.

Homes in both are frequently second homes, used seasonally or at weekends. That changes how they fill: not steadily, but in bursts, and then all of it gets dealt with at once when the place sells or a family decides to stop keeping it. What comes out is a decade of somebody’s weekends rather than a lifetime of daily living, which usually means better condition and more worth donating or reselling before the rest goes.

Both are managed communities, so give us the gate or the community name when you book and that part is handled before we come up.

Sky Country and the Mountain-View Lots

Sky Country sits at the highest elevation in Dahlonega, and the properties up there are bought for the view and the acreage that comes with it. Cavender Creek and the surrounding hills work much the same way.

Elevation has consequences for a haul. Drives are long, steep and often gravel, and they can be genuinely awkward for a loaded truck in bad weather. Houses are frequently built into the slope, so the lower level, the deck and whatever is stored under it are all below wherever a truck can safely stop. Everything has to come up.

That is the single thing a phone quote gets wrong up here. Tell us how steep the drive is and how far below the house sits, and we price the carry with the rest of it, once.
