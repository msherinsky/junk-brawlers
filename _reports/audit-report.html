Plain English: What is a Google Business Profile?

When you search for a business on Google and a box appears on the right side of the screen — or businesses appear on a map — that's the Google Business Profile. It's a separate listing that Google controls, not the website. Businesses can claim it, add photos, respond to reviews, and update their hours. It's free. The website (junkbrawlers.com) is something completely different that the business pays to host. Both exist independently, and Google looks at both when deciding who to show in search results.

Foundation
The Website and the 48-Review Google Listing Aren't Connected — and Google Knows It
What is Schema Markup?

Schema markup is a small invisible block of code that sits in the backend of a website. It doesn't change how the site looks to visitors. What it does is tell Google, in a very specific structured format: "This website belongs to Junk Brawlers LLC. Same business as the Google Maps listing. Here's the address, the phone number, the owner's name, the hours, the service area, and the 48 five-star reviews." It's essentially the official paperwork that formally links the website to everything else. Without it, Google sees the website and the GBP as two separate, unverified entities.

Right now, the website has no schema markup at all. That means every time Google evaluates junkbrawlers.com, it sees a site with decent content but no formal declaration of who it belongs to or what business it represents. Meanwhile, the Google Business Profile has 48 five-star reviews sitting there — but they're locked in the Maps listing and doing nothing for the website's rankings.

When Google decides who to show in search results for "junk removal Dawsonville GA," it looks for businesses where the website and the Google listing are clearly speaking the same language. Competitors with schema markup have that confirmation. Junk Brawlers doesn't — so Google ranks the others first, even when the reviews and service quality are better.

Schema audit — every page on junkbrawlers.com scanned LocalBusiness (declares the business to Google): Missing
AggregateRating (connects the 48 reviews to the website): Missing
FAQPage (helps AI search engines like ChatGPT surface the business): Missing
Organization (declares the brand identity): Missing

Result: Google cannot formally verify this website belongs to Junk Brawlers LLC

The fix: Add a LocalBusiness schema block to every page — this formally connects the website to the GBP and tells Google the address, phone, hours, and service area. Add AggregateRating schema so the 48 five-star reviews show up alongside search results. Add FAQPage schema so AI search engines (Google's AI Overview, ChatGPT, Perplexity) can pull Junk Brawlers into direct answers when people ask questions like "who does junk removal in Dawsonville Georgia?"

The Website Takes 8 Seconds to Load on a Phone — Most Visitors Leave in 3
What do these speed metrics mean in plain English?

Google measures page speed with five specific numbers. Each one measures a different part of the loading experience on a real mobile phone. Here's what each one means and where Junk Brawlers stands.

LCP — Largest Contentful Paint
8.0s
How long until the main content appears on screen. The page looks blank until this moment. Google wants under 2.5s. Junk Brawlers takes 8 seconds — 3× too slow.
CLS — Cumulative Layout Shift
0.692
How much the page jumps around while loading — buttons moving, text shifting, images popping in. Google wants under 0.1. Junk Brawlers scores 0.692 — 7× worse than acceptable.
TBT — Total Blocking Time
560ms
How long the page is completely frozen and won't respond to taps or clicks. Google wants under 200ms. Over half a second where nothing works.
FCP — First Contentful Paint
2.8s
How long until anything at all appears — even just a line of text. Google wants under 1.8s. Nearly 3 seconds of blank white screen before anything shows.
Why this directly costs leads: Google's own research shows 53% of mobile visitors leave a page if it takes more than 3 seconds to load. Junk Brawlers' page takes 8 seconds. That means for every 10 people who find the site on their phone, roughly 5–6 are gone before reading a single word. Speed is also a direct Google ranking factor — slower sites get pushed lower in search results, so fewer people find the site in the first place. It hurts twice.

The fix: Most of this is caused by the current platform — Network Solutions' website builder loads heavy scripts and stylesheets on every page that slow everything down. A rebuild on a faster platform would bring mobile scores into the 70–85+ range. Properly compressed images and cleaner code make the biggest difference.
