Bridgemill and Harmony Are Hitting Twenty

Bridgemill runs to roughly 3,000 homes around eighteen holes, and Harmony on the Lakes is not far behind it. Both went up in the early 2000s, which means the whole stock crosses twenty years at more or less the same time.

Twenty years is when a house starts costing money. Original appliances give out, builder-grade carpet finally goes, the bonus room gets redone, and the garage gets dealt with because it has been the overflow since move-in. That produces renovation debris and appliance turnover rather than estate work, and a lot of it on the same streets in the same seasons.

We take the old units and the tear-out in the same visit rather than making you book a debris crew separately. Nothing sits in the driveway waiting between the two.

The Etowah Estates, and Great Sky Still Going Up

Along the Etowah, River Green and Governors Preserve are a different market again: larger estate properties that turn over on their own timetable rather than a renovation cycle. Laurel Canyon runs up into the foothills, and Soleil is a gated 55+ community with its own downsizing rhythm.

Those are full clearances rather than upgrades. Several truckloads, often organised by family, and worth going through carefully before anything leaves.

Then at the other end Great Sky is still being built out across a lot of green space, which keeps generating the third kind of job: construction offcuts, packaging, and the crate a range arrived in. Three markets, one county seat, one crew.
